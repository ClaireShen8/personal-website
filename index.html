Air-Gapped Desktop Video Annotation Application

Built a locally run Python desktop application that takes a multi-hour video from scrubbing through to a training-ready dataset, with model-assisted pre-labeling and full traceability from every image back to its source frame, vastly optimizing the data labeling workflow

The Problem

The lab I interned at had a major, ongoing computer vision project that ran inference on real-time video feed. The models they had trained were effective, but the data labeling process was gradually becoming an insurmountable bottleneck. The training video footage, acquired off-site, could be as short as 15 minutes or multiple hours long. So, software engineers took hours out of the week to manually scrub through long videos, crop events into smaller clips, and mark coordinates frame by frame. Working with sensitive data meant that the entire process needed to be air-gapped (completely isolated from the cloud, managed locally). This made dataset management the most difficult of all. Multiple people had worked on the labeling effort over the years, without any standardized folder structure or labeling format — making it difficult to trace labeled data to its source, discern which data was labeled/unlabeled, and generate new datasets to train more accurate models.

With only 4 weeks left in my internship, I turned a 4-hour weekly labeling assignment into a new project sprint: building an offline application that streamlined the entire video-labeling lifecycle, from high-speed scrubbing and AI-assisted annotation to standardized, YOLO-ready dataset export.

What I Built

I started by interviewing each of the engineers who had worked on labeling, to understand the pain points. Then, I built a single locally run desktop application in Python with Qt and OpenCV that owns the whole path. The user scrubs video and marks events, extracts frames progressively at whatever density the model needs, and draws bounding boxes and keypoints that propagate across frames — correcting pose-estimation pre-labels rather than starting from an empty frame. Every exported image and label carries a reference back to its source video and exact frame, and datasets are written out in a standardized structure with event-aware train/validation/test splits.

My Impact

Labeling ran at roughly three times its prior speed, and every image and label became traceable back to its source video and exact frame — so the lab can tell what has already been labeled, regenerate a dataset on demand, and train new models without reconstructing that provenance by hand.

HouseMeds

Web tool · Prescription price search

A web tool that searches pre-vetted pharmacies for the cheapest way an entire household — pets included — can fill its prescriptions each month.

The Problem

Purchasing prescription medications is an expensive, time-consuming process for many Americans. In many cases, paying out of pocket rather than going through insurance is the easier and more affordable route — but finding out whether that's true for a given prescription means checking site after site, one drug at a time.

What I Built

A web tool that searches pre-vetted pharmacy websites for the most affordable way for an entire household, pets included, to fill its prescriptions each month — so the comparison happens once, across every medication in the house, instead of one drug and one site at a time.
